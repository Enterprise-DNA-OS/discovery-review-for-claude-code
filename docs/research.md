# Selection evidence

Checked 25 October 2026. New family: ediscovery. The registry already contains legal matter management, but no eDiscovery build. This run excludes marketing performance.

US$19,800 a year is twelve months of the published US$1,650 300 GB case plan at [GoldFynch pricing](https://goldfynch.com/pricing.html). The pricing page's slider arrays pair 300 with 1650. This is a constant-size annualised list-price scenario, not an actual NZ or AU customer's invoice. The vendor states that processing, productions and users are included. Do not describe its pricing as per-seat or invent add-on charges. Hosting location and contract arrangements require a quote.

Scores in the charter order: annual bill 3, data-shaped 4, our buyers 3, search demand 3, one-session buildable 4, import path 5. Total 22/30. The bill is near the 20k threshold; the three reflects that approximate bracket. Legal firms fit the existing buyer profile. No current GoldFynch customer invoice was located in the local business context. No live customer system was accessed. Search results show multiple alternative lists; keyword volume was not measured. These are conservative selection judgments, not demand measurements.

Other uncovered candidates: Silktide 3/3/3/3/2/4 = 18, with a larger crawler and browser-testing scope. Its [pricing page](https://silktide.com/pricing/) quotes privately; the UK public-sector schedule is not an ANZ quote. Nalpeiron 2/4/2/2/2/3 = 15, with application runtime integration as a substantial dependency; [published pricing](https://nalpeiron.com/pricing/) establishes a five-figure Core plan. Rights and royalties tools inspected had lower entry bills or quote-only larger installations, so did not establish a qualifying small-business invoice.

GoldFynch [report documentation](https://support.goldfynch.com/support/solutions/articles/2100034018-generating-reports-using-goldfynch-s-report-feature) supplies the import field names. The shipped example is synthetic. This is a bounded register-and-review replacement, not a complete eDiscovery processing engine. No claim is made that GoldFynch lacks the ten example analyses.
